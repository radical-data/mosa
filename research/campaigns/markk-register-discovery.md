# MARKK: discovery across the preserved Oceania registers

**Status: deferred thematic follow-up.** Catalogue-first coverage in the
programme index takes priority. This brief retains a reusable extraction and
reconciliation question for the two preserved MARKK registers.

## Question and rationale

What Rapa Nui material appears in MARKK's two published Oceania extracts beyond
the eight objects already followed from IndiGen? This offers accessible,
identifier-bearing evidence without another institution-access search. Tools,
featherwork, archaeological material and physical casts are priorities.

Reuse the [through-1920 source](../../collection/sources/markk-hamburg-oceania-2020.json)
and [from-1921 source](../../collection/sources/markk-hamburg-oceania-post1921.json),
including their registered originals. These are historical database extracts,
not evidence of current custody or an exhaustive present-day museum census.

## Scope and search plan

Plan dated 30 September 2026. Search the complete 1,220-page and 823-page
captures for `Rapa Nui`, `Rapanui`, `Osterinsel`, `Easter Island`, `Pascua`,
`Chile`, and material/designation variants such as `Obsidian`, `Netz`, `Feder`,
`Tapa`, `Holzplatte` and `Abguss`/`Abguß`. Material searches must be tied to
relevant geographic or documented collection context. Use the known eight
identifiers as positive controls. Inspect all geographic hits with their
column headings and page continuations; investigate blank or broad origin
fields in adjacent candidate rows without automatically assigning them to Rapa Nui.

The finite documentary boundary is both registered extracts. Text search
locates candidates but cannot establish that poorly encoded or image-only rows
are absent. Record search terms, page ranges inspected and any extraction gaps.
Visually check every row used for reconciliation. Work in reviewable batches,
then cover all relevant rows in both extracts; batch size does not limit the
scope.

Compare institution-scoped identifiers, including spaces and colon/dot
variants, against all MoSA sources and the Arte entries. Separate individual
objects, lots, components, casts and depictions. `Ozeanien/Polynesien/Chile`
is useful candidate context, but an insufficiently specific attribution must
remain unresolved rather than being silently rewritten as a findspot.

## Reconnaissance and candidate position

On 30 September 2026, whole-file text reconnaissance found Chile/Polynesia
rows beyond the already imported groups. Through-1920 page 168 and from-1921
page 397 were visually checked: E 997/E 998 and the 58.34 group provide promising
tool and featherwork leads. No exact E 997 or 58.34 accession string was found
in collection records in this planning check. This is not full reconciliation.
The rows need island-attribution and object-boundary review before import.

Whole-file keyword hits also included irrelevant uses of “Eastern” and a
moai-shaped item attributed to Hawaii. Such hits demonstrate why search counts
cannot be reported as Rapa Nui object counts. The two remote PDF URLs returned
errors through the web reader during this check; the registered local originals
were readable and used instead. No new capture is required for that reason.

## Integration and next action

Resume with the promising tool and featherwork rows, including E 997/E 998 and
the 58.34 group, and test whether museum documentation supplies sufficiently
specific Rapa Nui attribution. Then extract and review all relevant rows in
both registered files, recording useful page locators and gaps. Existing
captures support extraction; put source-specific interpretation in their
source notes. Do not infer image reuse from a blank or populated image column.
Account for individual objects, lots, components, casts and depictions, and
retain uncertain geography as unresolved. Any follow-up of the eight IndiGen
objects stays in the existing register.
