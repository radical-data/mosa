# Valparaíso beyond the carved figures

## Question and rationale

Which individually documented textiles, featherwork, tools and other objects
at the Museo de Historia Natural de Valparaíso are absent from MoSA, and which
existing records gain object-specific Rapanui testimony from the same sources?
This is a deferred thematic investigation: mainland Chile is in scope, the
source is already preserved, and the existing catalogue lookup works. Systematic
Chile Patrimonios coverage has priority in the programme index.

The [2017 article source](../../collection/sources/ramirez-2017-coleccion-mhnv.json)
currently contributes claims to only three objects. Its selective extraction
does not exhaust the publication. Reuse its registered PDF and the
[Chilean catalogue recipe](../../.agents/skills/identify-objects/references/holder-lookups.md#chile-patrimonios-and-surdoc).

## Scope and search plan

Plan dated 30 September 2026. First review all 25 PDF pages, including captions,
numbered specimens and the concluding collection discussion. Reconcile every
individually described candidate with the entire collection, not only the three
objects linked to the article. Treat museum inventory numbers and SURDOC
numbers as different schemes; verify any crosswalk using the object record,
photograph or distinctive description.

Then search the official Chile Patrimonios/SURDOC routes for the candidate's
exact identifiers and museum. Where necessary use `Rapa Nui`, `Rapanui`,
`Isla de Pascua`, `mahute`, `bandera`, `textil`, `plumas`, `toki`, `azuela`,
`mataa` and `anzuelo`, retaining accents and encountered spelling variants.
For each submitted search, record its institution filter, displayed count and
all pages inspected; review all results of the exact-identifier query. Broader
discovery across the portal is a subsequent pass, not a prerequisite for
finishing the article.

Use manageable integration batches, prioritising useful evidence about
underrepresented materials and functions. Continue through all individually
described candidates in the article, retaining locators for unresolved entries.
The article pass records a disposition for every candidate and states which
source preservation, claim and image-rights work is complete or remains.

## Reconnaissance and candidate position

On 30 September 2026, the preserved PDF's text was searched for numbered
figures, textiles and tools; page 12 was also visually reviewed. Figure 3
identifies the flag as inventory 197. It is a priority candidate, not a completed
identity match. No `bandera` description was found in the collection during
this planning check; compare photographs and identifier variants before
concluding that it is new. The source's existing notes explain why general
quotations attributed to Leonardo Pakarati cannot be assigned to objects by
their order in the article.

The museum's [collection overview](https://www.mhnv.gob.cl/colecciones/coleccion-etnografica-rapa-nui)
was also checked. Its approximate 250-object total motivates investigation;
it is not a target or a basis for subtracting MoSA's current records to predict
new additions. Full article reconciliation and catalogue pagination remain
unperformed in this planning pass. No objects or sources were added.

## Next action and expansion decision

Begin with the flag's page-12 caption and the associated discussion, then the
numbered tools in the later pages. Reuse the publication source for further
faithful claims. Keep unclear groups intact and preserve the speaker chain for
testimony. Review photograph permissions separately from the preserved PDF.

Continue through the remaining article candidates when individual identities
can be supported. Record geographic provenance, cultural attribution and
evidence role independently, following the [research guide](../../docs/research.md#discovery-strategy).
The article and its testimony can provide valuable source enrichment even when
they do not establish a new artefact's island origin. Fonck and Santiago are
separate future scopes; their holdings must not be silently included. If a
catalogue match fails, the article can still support an individual historical
object where its evidence is sufficient; current custody remains a separate
question.
