# Research programme

Use the [collection workflow](../docs/collection-publication.md) for ordinary
imports. Use a campaign when a question spans several sessions or needs a
shared next action; the [research guide](../docs/research.md) explains the small
set of responsibilities. The [template](campaigns/template.md) is optional.

## Strategy and baseline

Prioritisation reviewed on **30 September 2026**. Start with systematic
exploration of relevant digital museum catalogues, completing the agreed
catalogue scope across departments, search variants, pagination and relevant
record types before thematic deep dives. Catalogue coverage is exploratory
discovery; exact-object follow-up is a separate task for resolving known
candidates and identifiers. Batch size manages review and integration workload,
not the ambition or boundary of the agreed catalogue scope.

For each candidate, track geographic provenance, cultural or maker attribution,
and evidence role independently; “from Rapa Nui” and “about Rapa Nui” can
overlap. The [research guide](../docs/research.md#discovery-strategy) defines
these dimensions and how to retain source uncertainty.

Prioritise batches by expected supported new objects, source and evidence
enrichment, and information gain per unit of effort, using MoSA's existing
records, proven access routes and prior search outcomes. Reuse available APIs,
exports and lookup recipes. Do not repeat a failed route unless access, method
or evidence has changed. Identifiable additions, underrepresented materials
and functions, and work by named Rapanui makers and knowledge holders remain
important outcomes. A lack of publishable photographs does not exclude
well-documented objects.

The planning snapshot contains 587 object files and 264 source files. Of those
objects, 314 have claims only from the Arte table and/or IndiGen gallery.
These figures count repository records, including material on Rapa Nui and
other existing scope exceptions; they are not a census of displaced objects.
They were derived by grouping every source claim by `objectId` and comparing
each object's contributing source IDs with the two seed IDs. Recompute from
the collection when revisiting strategy; this is a dated rationale, not a
maintained dashboard.

The IndiGen register at revision 45 records 231 verified identities out of 417,
with 34 not-found, 50 blocked and 102 deferred outcomes. That history argues
against another indiscriminate accession-search pass. The Australian Museum
campaign demonstrates a more productive route: publications and acquisition
accounts have expanded its representation to twenty records, including
contemporary work and archaeological material. Its remaining obstacles now
largely require specific inventory or identifier evidence.

## Catalogue coverage priorities

The first active exploratory campaign is the British Museum digital
collection: [systematic catalogue coverage](campaigns/british-museum-digital-collection.md).
MoSA already has individual British Museum records, including Hoa Hakananai'a,
and existing exact-record evidence. No exploratory catalogue search preceded
this campaign. The first broad search sets have now been captured and initial
accession and representation batches are being integrated; the campaign owns
the dated coverage and remaining work. Known records and authority results are
entry points, not evidence of catalogue completeness.

The following catalogues have proven object-level routes or existing records.
The order is a planning judgement based on demonstrated access, likely useful
coverage and effort; none has a documented exhaustive exploratory search in
this programme. Existing identity work and exact-object checks do not establish
catalogue-wide coverage.

| Order | Catalogue | Evidence for a useful route | Coverage uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

The active campaign records full-page capture coverage and initial imports. | Search hits mix artefacts, representations and incidental mentions. The island authority omits narrower-locality records, including Hoa Hakananai'a; broader reconciliation and source review remain in progress. |
| 2 | [Te Papa](https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/) | Existing object records and a documented catalogue recipe; FE010655 is a positive control. The deferred brief also records the readable FE010656 detail and maker page. | IndiGen follow-up covers selected gallery-linked identities. Complete maker-related results and the wider catalogue scope have not been exhausted. |
| 3 | [Chile Patrimonios / SURDOC](https://www.chilepatrimonios.gob.cl/) | Multiple official fichas and linked SURDOC records are already integrated; a tested recipe documents DOI variation and exact search. | Existing records and the Valparaíso publication are selective. No systematic search across relevant institutions, material terms and pagination is complete. |
| 4 | [The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection) | Existing records and an established accession-to-catalogue lookup recipe provide positive controls. | IndiGen work follows selected gallery entries. Relevant catalogue departments, query variants and result pagination remain uncharted. |
| 5 | [Swedish National Museums of World Culture](https://collections.smvk.se/) | A tested Carlotta search route and a verified Etnografiska object record are documented. | Exact-record work and selected IndiGen entries do not establish coverage across either Carlotta collection or its search variants. |
| 6 | [Wereldmuseum Leiden](https://collectie.wereldculturen.nl/) | An existing Leiden record and tested fuzzy accession search with a stable handle are documented. | Known-record lookup is established; broader geographic/material discovery, result variants and pagination remain unassessed. |
| 7 | [Übersee-Museum Bremen](https://bremen.museum-digital.de/) | An existing Bremen record and tested museum-digital accession route are documented; the MARKK brief preserves adjacent but separate register leads. | Known-record resolution and a thematic lead do not establish catalogue coverage; relevant queries and pagination remain unassessed. |

Use manageable batches to review and reconcile discoveries as work proceeds,
then continue through the whole agreed catalogue scope. Compare supported new
object identities, source/evidence enrichment and information gained against
effort. Record candidate dispositions and integration limits; keep
image rights review separate from identity. A small sample helps schedule
review but does not count as coverage of the agreed catalogue scope.

For candidates already tracked by IndiGen, keep their follow-up stages in that
register; the new campaigns own only wider discovery coverage and links to
outcomes. Do not duplicate the register. Successful new institutional discoveries
can also resolve Arte entries, but proximity or matching types alone cannot
justify merging established handles.

The [Pacific-Studies directory](https://www.pacific-studies.net/geodetail.php?id=317)
and [Splendid Isolation](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/splendid-isolation-art-of-easter-island)
remain supplementary routes for institutions and collecting networks. The
Met's publication page was checked on 30 September 2026; its full catalogue was
not reviewed in that planning pass. Neither resource is an inventory-completeness
claim. Use them to identify further catalogue scopes after the listed proven
catalogues are assessed.

Campaigns contain scope, unresolved questions and next actions. Published facts
and source-specific findings stay in collection sources; shareable captures stay
in `source-files/`. Private material and scratch files stay in `research-local/`.
Retain useful research evidence only under the guide’s retention rule.
Research files do not enter the website.
